Speaking: Environmental Responsibility

1) Read Aloud: Protest Speech

📢 Instructions:

Read the text aloud. Focus on clear pronunciation, natural rhythm, and intonation. Don't worry about speed.

Good afternoon. We are here today outside the headquarters of NovaPack Solutions to say clearly: enough is enough. For years, this company has promised to reduce its environmental impact. It has published glossy reports about sustainability and corporate responsibility. But behind the marketing, the reality tells a different story.

NovaPack continues to manufacture billions of single-use plastic containers every year. Its factories release significant carbon emissions, yet the company refuses to publish transparent data about its supply chain. We are tired of greenwashing. We are tired of executives who talk about commitment while profits always come first.

We demand action. We demand a credible plan to cut waste, invest in renewable energy, and take accountability for the damage already done. We demand that business travel be reduced, that unnecessary packaging be eliminated, and that environmental regulation be respected - not treated as an inconvenience.

This is not radical. It is basic responsibility. Companies like NovaPack cannot claim to support the environment while shifting the cost of pollution onto communities and future generations. We will not be silent. We will keep protesting until real change begins.

2) Pronunciation Focus

A. Noun vs Verb Stress

In English, many words change stress depending on whether they are a noun or a verb. French speakers often keep the same stress pattern for both, which can sound unnatural.

👉 Tip: Nouns often stress the first syllable; verbs often stress the second.

PRO-test (noun) → pro-TEST (verb)  |  IN-crease (noun) → in-CREASE (verb)

Word Noun Verb Example
protest PRO-test pro-TEST We organised a PRO-test. / We will pro-TEST until they act.
increase IN-crease in-CREASE a sharp IN-crease in emissions / emissions in-CREASE every year
export EX-port ex-PORT plastic EX-ports / to ex-PORT waste overseas
permit PER-mit per-MIT a building PER-mit / to per-MIT excessive pollution
record RE-cord re-CORD public RE-cords / to re-CORD emissions accurately

B. Stress in Long Environmental Words

Environmental and corporate vocabulary often contains long words with endings in -tion, -ity, and -ment. Getting the stress wrong can make advanced vocabulary difficult to understand.

Word Syllables Stress pattern
sustainability 6 = sus : tain : a : BIL : i : ty o o o O o o
accountability 6 = a : ccount : a : BIL : i : ty o o o O o o
regulation 4 = reg : u : LA : tion o o O o
emissions 3 = e : MIS : sions o O o
renewable 4 = re : NEW : a : ble o O o o
biodegradable 6 = bi : o : de : GRAD : a : ble o o o O o o
commitment 3 = com : MIT : ment o O o

C. Intonation for Persuasion

In a persuasive speech or protest, intonation can help you sound more confident and emphatic. Stress the most important content words.

Practise these lines from the text:

We are tired of greenwashing.

We demand a credible plan to cut waste.

We will keep protesting until real change begins.

This is not radical. It is basic responsibility.

3) Discussion Questions

Give detailed answers with examples where possible.

  1. Is it fair to use taxes or financial penalties to discourage businesses from generating waste and carbon emissions? What are the risks and benefits of this approach?

  2. Should business travel be restricted, taxed, or replaced with virtual meetings as a matter of environmental policy? What industries would be most affected?

  3. How much responsibility lies with corporations compared with consumers and governments? Can individuals make a meaningful difference if large companies do not change?

  4. What is "greenwashing", and why is it a problem? How can customers, employees, or regulators identify when a company's environmental claims are misleading?

  5. Should workplaces eliminate single-use plastics, excessive packaging, and unnecessary printing? What practical challenges would companies face in making these changes?

  6. Can a company remain highly profitable while genuinely prioritising environmental responsibility? Or is there an inevitable conflict between profit and sustainability?

  7. What role should employees play in pushing their employer to adopt more responsible environmental practices? When is it appropriate to protest or speak out publicly?

  8. How should companies measure and report progress on sustainability? What information should be transparent to the public, investors, and regulators?

4) Mini Presentation: Opinion & Justification

Topic:

To what extent do you agree with the following statement?

"Large companies should be legally required to reduce their carbon emissions by a fixed percentage every year. Environmental taxes and financial penalties are the most effective way to enforce this, even if it reduces short-term profits."

Task:

Prepare and deliver a 2–3 minute presentation.

You must:

  • State clearly whether you agree or disagree
  • Give reasons and examples
  • Acknowledge the opposite point of view
  • End with a clear final opinion
