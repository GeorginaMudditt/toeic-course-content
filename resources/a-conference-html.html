Script

Have you ever wondered what it would take to make our organisations and communities genuinely more sustainable, rather than simply more sustainable in theory? If so, the Sustainable Futures Conference, taking place in Glasgow this November, could be well worth attending.

Bringing together professionals, researchers and community representatives from a wide range of sectors, the conference will explore how we can turn ambitious commitments into practical, long-term action. Rather than focusing exclusively on environmental issues, the programme will examine the connections between sustainability, economic development, public wellbeing and social inclusion.

Over the course of two days, delegates will have the opportunity to attend presentations, participate in discussions and exchange ideas with people working in very different fields. Topics will include the future of sustainable cities, responsible consumption, the changing world of work, and the role of communities in creating lasting change.

One of the conference's central aims is to encourage participants to look beyond their own professional environments and consider how different sectors can learn from one another. Whether you work in education, healthcare, business, local government or the creative industries, there should be something to take away.

The conference will be held at the Glasgow Convention Centre on the 12th and 13th of November. Places are limited, and early registration is recommended. For further information, including the full programme and registration details, visit the conference website.

Writing #1: Elevating Simple Ideas

The sentences below communicate the basic ideas, but they are rather simple, repetitive or fragmented.

Rewrite each example in a more sophisticated way. Try to combine ideas, vary your sentence structures and use more precise vocabulary where appropriate.

You do not need to use every piece of information exactly as it appears, but your version should preserve the original meaning.

Example 1: Introducing the conference

Simple version

There will be a conference in Glasgow. I want to go to the conference. At the conference there will be six different talks. I cannot decide which talks I want to go to.

Possible C1 rewrite

I am considering attending a forthcoming conference in Glasgow, which features six thought-provoking sessions on different aspects of sustainability. However, given the breadth of the programme, I am finding it difficult to decide which talks would be most worthwhile.

Language focus: Relative clauses, participle clauses, precise vocabulary, avoiding repetition.

Example 2: Explaining relevance

Simple version

I work in education. Sustainability is not directly related to my job. However, I think the conference could still be useful. Education can help people change their behaviour.

Possible C1 rewrite

Although sustainability is not directly connected to my professional responsibilities in education, I believe the conference could nevertheless offer valuable insights, particularly given the role education can play in encouraging lasting changes in behaviour.

Language focus: Concession (although, nevertheless), nominalisation, complex sentence structure.

Example 3: Expressing interest in a session

Simple version

I am interested in the session about the future of work. Many people are working differently now. Companies need to change. I want to know how they can do this.

Possible C1 rewrite

The session on the future of work particularly appeals to me, as the changing nature of employment is compelling organisations to rethink how they operate and support their employees. I would be interested to explore how businesses can adapt to these developments effectively.

Language focus: Appeal to, the changing nature of, compel, embedded questions, avoiding basic repetition.

Example 4: Expressing a contrasting viewpoint

Simple version

I think economic growth is important. However, it can damage the environment. Some people think we must choose one or the other. I do not agree with this.

Possible C1 rewrite

While economic growth remains essential to many societies, it can come at a considerable environmental cost. However, I am not convinced that economic prosperity and environmental responsibility are necessarily mutually exclusive.

Language focus: Concession, hedging, sophisticated contrast, mutually exclusive.

Example 5: Discussing practical challenges

Simple version

Many organisations say they want to be more sustainable. They make promises. However, they do not always do what they promise. This is a problem.

Possible C1 rewrite

Although many organisations publicly commit to becoming more sustainable, there is often a considerable gap between their stated ambitions and their actual implementation. Bridging this gap remains one of the key challenges facing businesses today.

Language focus: Commit to, stated ambitions, implementation, bridging the gap, nominalisation.

Example 6: Anticipating the outcome

Simple version

I hope the conference will give me some useful ideas. I want to learn about things I can use at work. I also want to meet people from other sectors. They may have different ideas.

Possible C1 rewrite

I hope the conference will provide not only practical ideas that could be applied in my own professional context, but also an opportunity to exchange perspectives with people from other sectors, whose experiences may offer valuable and unexpected insights.

Language focus: Not only… but also, relative clauses, exchange perspectives, professional context, unexpected insights.
