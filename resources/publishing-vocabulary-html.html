Vocabulary: Publishing

Part 1: Vocabulary List with Pronunciation and Translation

Word/Phrase Pronunciation French Translation Example Sentence
manuscript
🔊 Listen
 manuscrit She spent three years writing the manuscript before she let anyone else read it.
literary
🔊 Listen
 littéraire The novel won a literary prize for its beautiful writing and original story.
submission
🔊 Listen
 soumission (d'un manuscrit) His first submission was rejected, but a different publisher accepted the second one.
protagonist
🔊 Listen
 protagoniste Readers often sympathise with the protagonist, even when that character makes mistakes.
narrative
🔊 Listen
 récit A clear narrative helps the reader follow what happens from the first page to the last.
editorial
🔊 Listen
 éditorial The editorial team decides which books fit the publisher's list and how each one should be shaped.
copyright
🔊 Listen
 droit d'auteur The publisher asked the photographer to confirm she still held the copyright before they used her pictures on the cover.
blurb
🔊 Listen
 texte de quatrième de couverture A good blurb makes you want to open the book, without revealing how the story ends.
forthcoming title
🔊 Listen
 titre à paraître The publisher announced a forthcoming title by a well-known crime writer.
advance
🔊 Listen
 à-valoir She used part of her advance to take three months off work and finish the second draft.
royalties
🔊 Listen
 redevances Authors earn royalties each time a bookshop sells a copy of their book.
book launch
🔊 Listen
 lancement d'un livre The book launch included a short reading and time for readers to ask questions.
foreign rights
🔊 Listen
 droits étrangers Selling the foreign rights lets another publisher bring the book out in a different country.
translation
🔊 Listen
 traduction A good translation keeps the voice of the original book in a new language.
print run
🔊 Listen
 tirage A small print run is safer for a first novel, because unsold copies are expensive to store.
pitch
🔊 Listen
 pitch / argumentaire A strong pitch can persuade an editor to read the full story, not just the first page.
synopsis
🔊 Listen
 synopsis Editors often ask for a synopsis so they can see the whole plot before they read every chapter.
editing
🔊 Listen
 révision éditoriale The editing dragged on for weeks because the author and the editor could not agree on the last chapter.
proofreading
🔊 Listen
 correction d'épreuves During proofreading, someone noticed that the same character's name was spelled two different ways on page 214.
book fair
🔊 Listen
 salon du livre We met three Italian publishers at the Frankfurt book fair and promised to send them the manuscript the following week.

Part 3: Discussion Questions

Use the vocabulary from this worksheet to answer the following questions.

  1. What do you usually look for when you are buying a book?
  2. What are the advantages and disadvantages of reading a printed book vs an e-book?
  3. Have you read many books in English? How do you find it compared to reading in your native language?
  4. A novel is first published in English. Why might the publisher also sell the foreign rights, and what does a translation need to do well?
  5. If you were a first-time author, would you rather receive a large advance or higher royalties? What are the risks of each?
  6. You are organising a book launch for a novel you love. What do you think could make it successful?

📝 Answer Key

Part 2: Gap Fill Exercise

  1. On publication day, the author read two pages aloud at the book launch and then signed copies for people in the queue.
  2. The narrative jumps between three decades, so the reader has to piece the family history together.
  3. During editing, the writer was asked to cut a side character and make chapter four easier to follow.
  4. The novel still existed only as a manuscript on the author's laptop: 90,000 words, and not yet a printed book.
  5. The publisher printed 4,000 copies in the first print run and waited to see if bookshops asked for more.
  6. The blurb printed on the back cover made the novel sound exciting, but it carefully hid the final twist.
  7. At the London book fair, hundreds of publishers set up stands so they can meet booksellers and show their new books.
  8. The guidelines explain how to prepare a submission: a short letter, a one-page summary, and the first three chapters.
  9. After the book had earned back that first payment, the writer began to receive royalties of 8% on every copy sold.
  10. Standing by the editor's stand, the agent gave a two-minute spoken pitch about why commuters would buy this novel.
  11. At the weekly editorial meeting, the team argued about whether the ending was too sad for their readers.
  12. During the proofreading process, Gail found some typos and missing commas in Chapter 3.
  13. Booksellers can already order the novel, even though it is still listed as a forthcoming title and will not reach the shops until October.
  14. The award is only for literary fiction, so cookbooks, textbooks and sports biographies cannot be entered.
  15. The agent sold the foreign rights to publishers in Italy and Korea, so each country can bring out its own edition.
  16. Without the author's permission, posting whole chapters online would break copyright regulations.
  17. Unlike the back-cover text, the synopsis gave away the ending so the editor could judge the whole plot.
  18. For most of the story, readers follow Maya, the protagonist, as she tries to save her family's restaurant.
  19. The translation took eight months, and Miguel found it challenging making some of the humour work in Spanish.
  20. The contract offered an advance of €12,000, paid in three parts before the book went on sale.
