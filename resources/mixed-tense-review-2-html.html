Exercise 1: Speaking (Questions 1–10)

  1. What do you do? > Present Simple (habit, routine, things that are permanent).
    Examples: I am an English teacher / I drive a taxi
  2. How would you describe your role to someone who has no knowledge of the industry you work in? > Present Simple (habit, routine, things that are permanent).
    Examples: I teach English to adults / I work in a shop that sells children's clothes
  3. Describe your typical workday. > Present Simple (habit, routine, things that are permanent) / Present Continuous (temporary projects, things happening at the moment).
    Examples: I work from home / I start work at 8am / This week I am working from the head office in Singapore
  4. Did you go to university? If so, what did you study? > Past Simple (in the past, finished).
    Examples: I went to Cambridge University / I graduated in 2016 / I studied Marketing
  5. What was your first job when you finished your studies? > Past Simple (in the past, finished).
    Examples: I worked in a shop / I didn't like my first job
  6. Have you ever worked in another country? > Present Perfect (life experience, until now).
    Examples: I have never worked in another country / I have worked as a teacher in Brazil and Japan.
  7. What has been your biggest professional achievement so far? > Present Perfect (life experience, until now).
    Examples: I have increased our customer base by 30% / We have developed a new marketing strategy that has improved our brand visibility.
  8. What has been the biggest challenge you have faced in your career so far? > Present Perfect (life experience, until now).
    Examples: I have managed a team with tight deadlines / I have adapted to rapid changes in a startup environment
  9. Where do you see your career in five years? > Future forms (plans and predictions: will, going to, Present Continuous for fixed arrangements).
    Examples: I'm going to qualify as a personal trainer / I will probably still be working in insurance.
  10. If you won millions of euros on the lottery, would you still work? > Second Conditional (hypothetical present/future: if + Past Simple, would + infinitive).
    Examples: If I won millions, I would not work full-time / I would volunteer for a charity instead.

Exercise 2: Grammar Definitions

➡️ What's the difference?

  • 1A: "We launch four new training courses every year."
  • 1B: "We launched the first Business English course last year."
  • 1C: "We have launched two courses this year."

1A uses the Present Simple for a habit or routine — something that happens regularly.

1B uses the Past Simple for a finished action at a specific time in the past (last year).

1C uses the Present Perfect to connect a past action to the present — the time period is unfinished (this year).

  • 2A: "If sales increase for three consecutive months, we hire new staff."
  • 2B: "If sales increase for three consecutive months, we will hire new staff."
  • 2C: "If sales increased for three consecutive months, we would hire new staff."

2A is the Zero Conditional (if + Present Simple, Present Simple) — a general rule or company policy that is always true.

2B is the First Conditional (if + Present Simple, will + infinitive) — a real, likely future consequence.

2C is the Second Conditional (if + Past Simple, would + infinitive) — a hypothetical or less likely situation.

  • 3A: "When I arrived, Martin started the presentation."
  • 3B: "When I arrived, Martin had started the presentation."
  • 3C: "When I arrived, Martin was starting the presentation."

3A uses the Past Simple in both clauses — Martin started the presentation when I arrived (the two actions happen at the same time, or one immediately follows the other).

3B uses the Past Perfect (had started) — Martin started the presentation before I arrived; the Past Perfect shows the earlier of the two past actions.

3C uses the Past Continuous (was starting) — Martin was in the middle of starting the presentation when I arrived.

Exercise 4: Speaking

Tell me about a project you are working on at the moment.

Discuss:

  • What you have achieved so far
  • What you haven't finished yet
  • What problems you have faced
  • What you have learned recently
  • What you are going to do next

At the moment, I'm working on rebranding my company. I've been working on it for the past few months because I wanted to give the business a more modern and professional image.

So far, I've redesigned the logo, updated the website and created a new visual identity for the company. I've also started using the new branding on social media and in my marketing materials.

However, I haven't finished everything yet. I still need to update some of my older documents and make sure that the new branding is consistent across all of my platforms.

I've faced a few problems along the way. For example, it's been quite difficult to decide exactly how I want the new brand to look, and I've had to make several changes to the original design. I've also found that rebranding takes much more time than I initially expected.

Through this project, I've learned a lot about how important consistency is when you're building a brand. I've also realised that it's important to think carefully about how you want customers to perceive your company.

Next, I'm going to finish updating the remaining materials and launch the new branding officially. I'm also planning to announce the rebrand on social media and ask my customers for feedback.
