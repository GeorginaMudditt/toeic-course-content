Speaking: Customer Service

1) Read Aloud: Customer Service Training Session

📢 Instructions:

Read the text aloud. Focus on clear pronunciation, natural rhythm, and intonation. Don't worry about speed.

Good morning, everyone, and welcome to today's customer service training session. Over the next few minutes, I want to share some ideas about what excellent customer service really means, and why it matters for every business.

Many people think customer service begins when something goes wrong. In reality, it starts the moment a customer first contacts your company. Whether they have a simple query, need after-sales support, or want to leave feedback, every interaction is an opportunity to build trust and improve satisfaction.

There are three principles I want you to remember. First, listen carefully before you respond. Customers who feel heard are far more likely to stay calm, even when they have a complaint. Second, be courteous at all times, even when the situation is difficult. A sincere apology and a clear plan to resolve the issue can turn a frustrated caller into a loyal client. Third, know when to escalate. Some problems require a supervisor or a specialist, and recognising that early saves time for everyone.

Finally, remember that customer satisfaction is not just about fixing problems. It's about creating an experience that encourages retention. When customers feel valued, they come back again and again - and they recommend your company to others. Thank you for your attention. Now, let's put these ideas into practice.

2) Pronunciation Focus

A. The "-ate" Ending: Verbs vs Adjectives

French speakers often pronounce "-ate" like /eɪt/ — like the number "eight". This is correct for verbs, but not for adjectives, where the ending is softer and unstressed: /ət/ (a short "ut" sound).

👉 Tip: Listen for the word type.

Verbs → stress on the final syllable: com-mu-ni-CATE (/keɪt/)

Adjectives → stress earlier, soft ending: ap-pro-xi-mate (/mət/), pas-sio-nate (/nət/)

Word Type Syllables "-ate" sound
communicate verb com : MU : ni : cate /eɪt/
appreciate verb ap : PRE : ci : ate /eɪt/
approximate adjective ap : PROX : i : mate /ət/
passionate adjective PAS : sion : ate /ət/
appropriate adjective ap : PRO : pri : ate /ət/
adequate adjective AD : e : quate /ət/
considerate adjective con : SID : er : ate /ət/

Practise these customer service phrases — notice the soft /ət/ endings:

We can give you an approximate delivery date.

Our team is passionate about customer satisfaction.

That seems like an appropriate solution for your situation.

B. False Friends (English ↔ French)

These words look similar in both languages but have different meanings. Using them incorrectly can cause confusion in customer service situations.

English word English meaning Looks like French…
actually in fact, really actuellement = currently, at the moment
eventually in the end, finally éventuellement = possibly, maybe
sensible reasonable, practical sensible = sensitive
assist to help someone assister = to attend (be present at)
demand to ask firmly / a strong request demander = to ask (neutral)

3) Discussion Questions

Give detailed answers with examples from your own experience where possible. Aim for at least 4–5 sentences per question.

  1. How is AI changing customer service today? What are the main advantages and risks of using chatbots and automated systems?

  2. Do you agree with the saying "the customer is always right"? Why or why not? Can you think of situations where this might be problematic?

  3. How does customer service differ between countries or cultures you have experienced? What expectations do customers have in France compared to other places?

  4. What makes a complaint difficult to handle? How should staff deal with angry or upset customers while remaining courteous?

  5. Should companies prioritise fast response times or personalised, human service? Is it possible to achieve both, and if so, how?

  6. How important is after-sales service for customer retention compared to the initial purchase experience? Can good after-sales service compensate for a disappointing product?

  7. Describe a time when customer service — either excellent or terrible — changed your opinion of a company or brand. What happened, and what was the outcome?

  8. What role should social media play in modern customer service? What are the risks of handling complaints publicly online?

  9. When a customer receives a faulty or defective product, should the company always offer a refund? When is an exchange or repair a better solution?

  10. How can companies use customer feedback to improve their service standards without ignoring individual complaints in the process?

4) Mini Presentation: Opinion & Justification

Topic:

To what extent do you agree with the following statement?

"For most businesses, keeping existing customers is more important than finding new ones. Companies should invest more in customer retention and after-sales service than in marketing and sales to attract new clients."

Task:

Prepare and deliver a 2–3 minute presentation.

You must:

  • State clearly whether you agree or disagree with the statement
  • Explain why customer retention matters
  • Give at least two concrete examples
  • Acknowledge the opposite view
  • End with a clear final opinion
